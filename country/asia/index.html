Why study in Asia

Asia and the Middle East offer world-ranked universities, branch campuses of UK and Australian universities, and lower living costs than many Western destinations, all a short flight from India.

Visa rules, work rights and costs differ a lot between countries. Choose one of our most popular Asian destinations below to see the essentials.

Study in Singapore

World-ranked universities, English as the language of teaching and a global business hub a short flight from India.

Main intakes
Aug (main), Jan
Work during study
16 hrs/week in term at approved institutions; full-time in vacations
Funds to show
Set by your institution; usually shown with bank statements or an education loan
After graduation
1-year Long-Term Visit Pass to look for work (you need a work pass to start a job)
  • Your institution starts your Student’s Pass application with the ICA
  • Work rights during term apply only at institutions approved by the Ministry of Manpower
  • Employers apply for your Employment Pass or S Pass after you get an offer

Study in UAE

Branch campuses of UK, Australian and Indian universities in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, close to home.

Main intakes
Sep (main), Jan, some May
Work during study
Part-time with a university NOC and MoHRE work permit (about 15–20 hrs/week in term)
Funds to show
Set by your university; usually tuition and a bank statement or sponsor letter
After graduation
10-year Golden Visa for top graduates (GPA 3.5+ from eligible universities); otherwise an employer-sponsored work visa
  • Your university usually sponsors your student residence visa
  • Branch campuses award the same degree as the home university
  • Full-time work is allowed during official breaks with a permit

Study in Japan

Technology, engineering and business programmes at leading universities, with more English-taught degrees every year.

Main intakes
Apr (main), Oct
Work during study
28 hrs/week with permission; up to 40 hrs/week in long vacations
Funds to show
Proof that you or a sponsor can cover tuition and living costs, checked for your Certificate of Eligibility
After graduation
Up to 1 year on a job-hunting visa (Designated Activities), with a university recommendation
  • Your school applies for a Certificate of Eligibility (CoE) before your visa
  • Apply for part-time work permission, often at the airport on arrival
  • Many courses are in Japanese and ask for a JLPT certificate

Study in Malaysia

Affordable English-taught degrees, including branch campuses of UK and Australian universities.

Main intakes
Jan–Feb and Sep, with some May starts
Work during study
20 hrs/week, only in semester breaks or holidays over 7 days
Funds to show
Set by your institution and EMGS; shown with bank statements
After graduation
No general post-study visa; staying on needs a job offer and an employment pass
  • Applications go through Education Malaysia Global Services (EMGS)
  • Work is limited to approved sectors such as restaurants and hotels
  • Living costs are among the lowest of our destinations

Study in South Korea

Strong engineering and technology universities, generous scholarships and a fast-growing English-taught offer.

Main intakes
Mar (main), Sep
Work during study
10–30 hrs/week on weekdays after 6 months, depending on your degree and Korean level (TOPIK)
Funds to show
About USD 18,000–20,000 in a bank balance, depending on the region
After graduation
D-10 job-seeker visa after graduation
  • Degree students apply for the D-2 student visa
  • The Global Korea Scholarship (GKS) covers tuition and living costs for selected students
  • A TOPIK certificate unlocks more work hours

English tests for Asia

Universities in Singapore, the UAE and Malaysia that teach in English usually accept IELTS or PTE. Japan and South Korea have English-taught programmes too, but many courses are taught in Japanese or Korean and ask for a JLPT or TOPIK certificate. We'll check what each of your shortlisted programmes asks for.

Official sources

Always check the government's own pages before you apply. These are the ones we use.

  • Work pass exemption for foreign students (Ministry of Manpower, Singapore)
  • Long-Term Visit Pass for graduates seeking work (ICA, Singapore)
  • Golden residence for top students and graduates (GDRFA Dubai)
  • Studying and working in Malaysia (Education Malaysia Global Services)
